For clinical laboratory science students & lab staff

1

What is free hemoglobin?

  • Free Hb = hemoglobin released from lysed red cells into plasma or serum (hemolysis). Normal plasma holds only traces.
  • Free Hb absorbs light strongly at ≈ 415 nm (Soret peak) and at 540–580 nm — the latter gives plasma its pink → red colour; both can disturb photometric tests.
  • Lysis also releases other cell contents (K+, LDH, AST, phosphate…).

Free Hb may come from the patient (intravascular hemolysis) or the tube — the analyzer cannot tell which. Deciding is a key lab skill (panel 2).

2

In-vivo vs in-vitro hemolysis

In vivo in the patient's circulation

A true clinical finding — report it. E.g., hemolytic transfusion reaction, immune/drug hemolysis, PNH, G6PD deficiency, microangiopathy (TTP/HUS/DIC), devices (valves, ECMO, VAD, bypass), malaria.

Clues: ↓ haptoglobin, ↑ LDH, ↑ unconjugated bilirubin, ↑ reticulocytes, hemoglobinuria; hemolysis persists on careful re-collection. Extravascular hemolysis may leave plasma clear.

In vitro during collection / handling

A pre-analytical artefact — the patient's blood is not hemolyzed. A frequent reason for sample rejection and recollection; results may be misleading if released unflagged.

Clues: haptoglobin normal; no hemoglobinuria; a properly re-collected sample is clear with normal markers.

4

How free Hb is measured

A · Spectrophotometry — e.g., Harboe method

  • Diluted plasma read at 415 nm (oxy-Hb Soret peak).
  • 380 & 450 nm → Allen-type baseline correction for linear background (mainly turbidity); marked icterus or lipemia can still interfere.
  • Quantitative: device monitoring, blood-unit QC.
  • Other methods exist — availability varies.

B · Automated hemolysis index (H-index) on chemistry analyzers

  • Objective & rapid on samples run with serum indices; flags affected assays per manufacturer interference claims.
  • Limits: semi-quantitative; cannot separate in-vivo from in-vitro; units & calibration differ between analyzers.

6

Clinical relevance intravascular hemolysis

Transfusion reactions

Acute hemolytic reaction (e.g., ABO-incompatible) → hemoglobinemia, hemoglobinuria; work-up compares post- vs pre-transfusion plasma. Non-immune lysis: improper warming/freezing, incompatible IV fluids, mechanical stress.

ECMO & devices

Shear in pumps, oxygenators and circuits (ECMO, VAD, bypass) lyses red cells. Serial PFH tracks hemolysis and can flag pump / circuit problems (e.g., thrombosis). Action thresholds are centre-specific.

Stored RBC units

Storage lesion: free Hb accumulates in the supernatant over shelf life. End-of-storage hemolysis is a QC parameter; limits are set by regulators / standards and differ by jurisdiction.
